Before anything else in a restoration project can work, the water has to be extracted. Structural materials, floors, possessions and building systems all take less damage when standing water comes out sooner. Left standing, water keeps moving deeper by the hour, wicking into drywall, soaking carpet pad, seeping under flooring and saturating the framing. Across Santee, Santee Restoration handles emergency extraction with industrial portable and truck-mounted machines that can remove hundreds of gallons a minute.

Few water removal systems match the power of a truck-mounted extractor, which draws water from structural materials, carpeting and hard flooring far better than rental or consumer machines. Confined spaces, wall cavities and the gaps between floor layers call for different gear, so we use portable extractors, specialty tools and submersible pumps in places the truck-mounted hoses cannot get to.

There is more to a professional extraction than getting rid of the water in plain sight. Our IICRC-certified technicians use moisture detection equipment to identify water that has migrated into hidden areas: behind baseboards, inside wall cavities, under cabinets and beneath flooring materials. Specialty extraction tools remove as much moisture as possible from these hidden areas before the drying phase begins, which significantly reduces the time and equipment needed for structural drying and lowers the risk of secondary mold growth.

Signs It Is Time for Water Extraction

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Water flowing or dripping from ceilings, walls or fixtures
  • Floors flooded by a fire sprinkler or suppression system going off
  • A utility area, mechanical room or crawl space with water in it
  • Seams in the floor that ooze water under foot pressure
  • An appliance, HVAC unit or pipe that is leaking right now
  • Water entering through a flooded basement stairwell or window well
  • A basement taking on water after heavy rain or a sump pump failure

How to Prevent Water Extraction Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Pour water into the sump pit a few times a year to confirm the pump kicks on
  • Make sure your home has working water leak sensors in critical areas
  • Keep valuables and stored boxes off the basement floor on shelves or pallets
  • Put together a simple kit with a bucket, some old towels and the phone number of your restoration company
  • Leak-sensing shutoff valves can cut off the water on their own when something fails
  • Maintain appliance supply lines and replace hoses that show wear or age
