Kitchen Water Damage Help in Santee

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. We help Santee homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

What It Looks Like

  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher
  • Vinyl floor that has discolored or gone soft by the cabinets or the refrigerator
  • The ceiling under the kitchen has developed a brown ring
  • Warping, staining or a soft spot in the floor of the cabinet below the sink
  • Cupped or raised floorboards, wood or laminate, near the dishwasher door
  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Day 3 to 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

Past one week

Removal Becomes Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.

How We Fix It

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Shut Off the Leak

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Map the Moisture

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry Without Demolition

With the toe kicks off, air gets pushed below the cabinets and behind them. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Toe Kick Space

Every side of the space beneath base cabinets is closed off. Water pools in it for weeks, and the mold that grows goes unseen until the cabinets come out.

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. A leak there wets drywall and framing that the cabinets cover completely, so mold can spread across the wall unseen.

Flooring Over Flooring

It is common for a kitchen floor to be laid right over the previous one. Water gets trapped between the layers, where it cannot evaporate and will not show up on the surface.

Behind the Dishwasher

A slow drip at the supply or drain connection wets the subfloor and the cabinet sides next to it. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.
